Exporting Pet Products to Russia: Contract to Payment

Even for the same pet product, what you need to prepare for Russia depends on the item, the buyer and the payment route. This guide lists the five steps a Korean pet brand goes through on its first export to Russia. Always confirm permits for each transaction with the relevant authorities in advance.

1. Contract: fix the terms in writing first

State the product name and HS code (pet food is usually 2309), quantity, unit price, Incoterms, currency and payment timing. Add clauses for sanctions changes or payment delays and agree on jurisdiction and governing law. For food and treats, also agree the minimum remaining shelf life on arrival in Russia.

2. Check that the product can be exported

Check whether the item is a strategic item or on the Korean list of goods requiring situational licences for Russia (HS 6-digit list), via KOSTI's yestrade.go.kr self-assessment or expert classification. For pet food and treats, the first step is to confirm Russia's export quarantine requirements and plant registration with Korea's Animal and Plant Quarantine Agency (see the next guide).

3. Export and import customs

Korea-side export declaration goes through Korea Customs' UNI-PASS; Russian import clearance is done by an importer registered in Russia. Prepare the invoice, packing list, transport documents, certificate of origin, veterinary certificate and any conformity papers as one set, and cross-check names, quantities and values before shipment — delays on the Korean side push back clearance in Russia too.

4. Choose the route

Cargo from Korea to Russia usually moves by sea–rail via Far Eastern ports such as Vladivostok, or by air. Pet food quality can be affected by temperature and humidity, so choose container and loading conditions for the season. Rates and transit times change often — confirm them at quotation.

5. Payment and collection

Banks differ in accepted currencies and checks for Russia-related payments, so settlement can take longer than expected. Ask your bank about routes and documents in advance, consider prepayment or instalments to reduce risk, and keep payment records per transaction.

Pet Food Import Rules in Russia: Plant Registration, Vet Documents, Marking

In Russia, pet food and treats fall under veterinary control, so the first question is not the product but the plant that made it. Add mandatory digital marking, and missing a step can stop both customs and sales. Supplements and accessories have different rules depending on classification and need item-by-item checks.

The plant must be on Russia's register

To export Korean pet food to Russia, the manufacturing plant must be listed in Rosselkhoznadzor's register of third-country establishments (Cerberus system). Foreign companies cannot apply directly; applications go through Korea's veterinary authority. So the first step is to ask Korea's Animal and Plant Quarantine Agency whether your plant is registered for Russia and what the export quarantine requirements are.

Veterinary certificate and Mercury e-documents

Each shipment travels with a veterinary certificate from the exporting country, and inside Russia the movement of imported pet food is tracked with electronic veterinary documents in the Mercury system (VSD). Certificate formats and additional papers (such as a declaration of conformity) can vary by product and over time, so confirm them before shipment.

Chestny ZNAK marking is mandatory

In Russia, dry food and treats (HS 2309) have required Chestny ZNAK digital marking since October 2024, wet food since March 2025. Since September 2025 codes are scanned at checkout; since November 2025 registrations are reconciled against Mercury VSD quantities; from December 2026 item-level traceability begins. Plan code application at the packing stage, as imported goods must be marked before clearance.

Supplements and accessories: check classification first

Pet supplements follow different procedures depending on whether they are classed as feed, feed additives or veterinary medicines. Feed additives require state registration with Rosselkhoznadzor, under a new procedure since September 2025. For accessories such as toys, hygiene products or clothing, the applicable EAC technical regulation varies, so confirm with a certification body first. The EAEU feed-safety technical regulation is still a draft as of November 2026 and may change requirements once adopted.

How to Check Russia Sanctions and Export Controls

For Russia-related deals, check both what you ship and to whom. Most pet products are ordinary consumer goods, but counterparties and payment routes must be screened regardless of the item. This covers basic checks with official tools; final judgement needs prior confirmation from authorities or experts.

Korea: check the item

Apart from strategic-item status, check whether goods for Russia or Belarus are on the situational-licence list, defined by HS 6-digit codes and updated with each notice. Get a self or expert classification on KOSTI's yestrade.go.kr and keep the record.

Screen counterparties on official lists

Korea offers the yestrade.go.kr end-user search; the US offers OFAC Sanctions List Search and the Consolidated Screening List (trade.gov); the EU offers the EU Sanctions Map — all free. Screen buyers, importers, carriers and paying banks by name and address, and verify near matches with registration details.

Red flags and records

Purchases that don't fit the buyer's business, above-market prices or vague third-country routing are typical red flags. Keep classifications and screening results with dates, and re-screen before each new contract or shipment.

Selling Online in Russia: Wildberries, Ozon and Logistics

Russian pet owners buy much of their food, treats and supplies on marketplaces such as Wildberries, Ozon and Yandex Market. Seller registration, pet product documents and logistics have Russia-specific requirements to prepare for. Check each platform's official seller guidance for details.

Who is the seller

Marketplace registration requires Russian business details, so the usual model is for a Russian importer to import and clear the goods and list them on its own seller account. Korean brand owners should agree trademark use, pricing and supply terms with the importer by contract; registering the trademark in Russia helps against copies.

Documents and marking for pet products

Food and treats are under veterinary control, so marketplaces also check Mercury e-documents and Chestny ZNAK codes at intake. Even for items exempt from certification, a marketplace may ask for an exemption letter. Prepare Russian product content and labels (ingredients, manufacturer, importer, shelf life) to Russian standards.

Logistics: not only marketplace warehouses

There are two main models: stock placed in marketplace warehouses and delivered by the platform (FBO), or stock kept in the seller's warehouse and shipped on each order (FBS). FBO is faster but requires strict intake schedules and storage rules; FBS is more flexible but the seller handles dispatch. Storage and logistics fees change often — check the seller portals.

A key advantage with PET LEGEND is access to our Russian subsidiary's own warehouse in addition to marketplace fulfilment centres. Keep stock there, send fast movers to marketplace warehouses and fulfil the rest via FBS — avoiding intake-schedule pressure and spreading inventory and storage costs. The same stock also serves offline customers such as pet shops, vet clinics and franchises.

Wildberries, Ozon, Yandex Market

All three run both warehouse and seller-fulfilled models, but seller requirements, fee structures and how they check pet category documents differ and change frequently. We recommend starting small on one or two platforms, then expanding channels and stock once sales are steady.
